# Sophie the Stegosaurus — knowledge pack

Every section ends with its source. Only sourced statements may be used as facts in narration.
Statements marked UNSOURCED are context for the writer and must not be stated as fact.

## Who she is
Sophie is a Stegosaurus stenops, 150 million years old.
Source: https://www.nhm.ac.uk/press-office/press-releases/world_s-most-complete-stegosaurus-goes-on-show.html

She lived about 150 million years ago, in the Jurassic Period, in what is now Wyoming, USA. She is one of the most
complete Stegosaurus skeletons ever found; most Stegosaurus skeletons that are found are incomplete. She is on
display in the museum's Earth Hall.
Source: https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/stegosaurus-brought-to-life.html

## Discovery
She was found in spring 2003 at Red Canyon Ranch, Wyoming, by Bob Simon, who runs a dinosaur quarry there.
He found her nearly fully connected skeleton buried at the top of a hill while expanding the quarry, and excavation took three weeks.
Source: https://www.nhm.ac.uk/press-office/press-releases/world_s-most-complete-stegosaurus-goes-on-show.html

## Completeness and size
She was found virtually complete; only the left arm and the base of the tail are missing. She is 560 cm long
and 290 cm tall, about the size of a 4x4, with over 300 bones. Her 19 back plates and four tail spikes are the
most complete set ever discovered.
Source: https://www.nhm.ac.uk/press-office/press-releases/world_s-most-complete-stegosaurus-goes-on-show.html

## Her skull
Her skull bones are three-dimensional and separate from one another, making it one of the most scientifically
valuable dinosaur skulls ever found, useful for studying how she ate and how hard she bit.
Source: https://www.nhm.ac.uk/press-office/press-releases/world_s-most-complete-stegosaurus-goes-on-show.html

## Coming to London
She arrived at the museum in December 2013. The museum's director thanked 70 generous donors, with particular
thanks to Jeremy Herrmann, for making the acquisition possible. She is named in honour of his daughter, Sophie
Herrmann. She went on display inside the Exhibition Road entrance from 4 December 2014, the first complete dinosaur
specimen to go on display at the museum in nearly 100 years. At the time of this 2014 press release she was the
only Stegosaurus in a public collection outside the USA; this may no longer be true, so do not
state it as a present-day fact. Her sex is not known; she was a young adult when she died.
Source: https://www.nhm.ac.uk/press-office/press-releases/world_s-most-complete-stegosaurus-goes-on-show.html

## How heavy she was
Scientists built a digital model of her whole skeleton and each of her 360 bones. After allowing for the fact
that she was a young adult whose limbs were still growing faster than her body, two methods agreed she weighed
about 1,600 kg in life, roughly a small rhino.
Source: https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2015/march/weight-of-the-worlds-most-complete-stegosaurus-revealed.html

## How she lived
She walked on all fours. Her front legs were short and back legs long, so she could not run and walked slowly at
only a few miles an hour. Her bite was about as strong as a cow's or sheep's, and she probably ate ferns, cycads
and other tough plants. Her small head had teeth about the size of a little fingernail.
Source: https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/stegosaurus-brought-to-life.html

## Her plates
The plates on her back are very large but thin and fragile. Defence is now thought unlikely; they may have been
for display or for controlling body temperature; the plates seem to have been filled with lots of blood vessels. The space in her hips stored
food substances; it was not a second brain.
Source: https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/stegosaurus-brought-to-life.html

## Bone count (conflicting)
The press release says over 300 bones; the 2015 news story says 360. Use "over 300" if a number is needed.

## Completeness percentage (UNSOURCED)
Press coverage gives figures of 80-90% complete. No NHM page read gives a percentage; do not state one.

## Appearance (for visuals, not narration)
A dark brown fossil skeleton mounted on a low plinth in the Earth Hall, a tall atrium at the Exhibition Road
entrance. She stands on four legs with short front limbs and long hind limbs, so her back arches high over the
hips; a small low head on a short neck; a double row of large, thin, diamond-shaped plates along her spine; four
long spikes near the tail tip. In life (reconstructions, not known for certain): a heavy, slow, rhino-sized
plant eater in a Jurassic landscape of ferns and cycads.
