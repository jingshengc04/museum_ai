##### centre
NEW CENTRE: "Make the exhibit act." (让展品活过来，再让它回到原位。)

The harness's first question changes from "what could go wrong?" to "what does this object do when it comes alive, and what does the visitor feel when they look up at it afterwards?" Every film is a 15 s (or 2 x 15 s) live-action fantasy beat in which the exhibit is the main character. A technically clean film in which the exhibit never acts now counts as a failure. The first Zun film (push-ins over a motionless bronze) is the negative anchor and should score aliveness 1-2.

Truth follows two separate rules.
- SAID: strict and unchanged. Every spoken claim cites fact ids that say it, in the pack's wording, with no added qualifiers, no worked-out figures, and nothing from appearance notes, UNSOURCED material or conflict notes.
- SHOWN: free, but it must read as fantasy (waking, breathing, leaping, the world bending, a passage through the glass). It must never be staged as a historical record: no documentary-register casting, excavation, bombing, ritual with priests, named or identifiable people, or a site or hall the object was never in. A glimpse of the past is allowed only in one stated stylised look (ink-wash, shadow puppet, rubbing, the object's own decoration come alive) or as the object's memory entered through a transition device, and no spoken line may present that image as what happened.

Renderability stops being a ban on motion. It becomes the set of Seedance's real failure modes:
- several movers at once
- more than one transformation per shot
- stacked camera moves
- instruction overflow
- references with no stated job, or two references that disagree about the same subject
- orbiting onto sides no reference shows
- text in frame
- long lip-synced lines

THREE MODES. The agent picks one per exhibit and may combine two: the main mode gives the big beat and the other gives one shot.

1. AWAKEN (default when the object has a figurative part: an animal, figure, face or creature). One hero mover wakes in place, acts with a want, causes a physical consequence, and is back in place before we look.
2. WORLD RETURNS (default when nothing on the object can plausibly move: a plain vessel, coin, manuscript or tool). The object stays perfectly still while its world, light, contents, time and scale move fast around it. It reads as the object's memory or a fantasy passage and returns by match cut.
3. CHARACTER (layered on 1 or 2 when the visitor's photo invites direct address). The awakened part has a personality, notices the visitor, and may speak short in-character lines (voice lines with no claims, or sourced facts as a boast or a confession).

Choosing a mode:
- Does the object have one part that could plausibly move and be filmed whole again? Use AWAKEN.
- Is its function (holding, pouring, sailing) the strongest image? Use WORLD RETURNS, with the CONTENTS moving.
- Did the visitor frame a face or a creature head-on? Add CHARACTER.

FIXED SHAPE of every 15 s generation: STILL, then STIR, then ACT, then RETURN.
1. The real exhibit, grounded, with a first sign of life by about 3 s.
2. The inciting physical change.
3. The high-amplitude beat: a speed or force word, a physical result, and no voice over it.
4. Back to the real exhibit at the visitor's photo angle, whole and correct, with one trace of life left behind.

The voice is a terse sourced counter-track of 15-22 words per 15 s. Sound is the second actor.

PASS GATE (code):
- no hard-check failures and no blocking issues
- truth = 5
- aliveness >= 4, spectacle >= 4, return >= 4
- fantasy_legibility is pass or fail (a fail is blocking)
- every other score >= 3

checks.py, seedance.py, schemas.py and prompts S1b, S2, S3 and S4 must change in the same commit. The old MULTI_MOVE check, the mixed-state check, the "no morphing" line and S4's "no fast motion, transformations" each veto the new films on their own, so a partial change will produce contradictory feedback loops.

Schema additions this centre needs (schemas.py):
- Concept: mode, hero_mover, want (5 words), first_sign, big_move, return_trace, spectacle_shot.
- Script: mode, hero_mover, hero_want, still_parts (e.g. 'the other three rams'), look (one concrete look line), return_trace.
- ShotSpec:
  - beat: still | stir | act | return
  - transition_in: cut | match cut on <feature> | whip pan | through the glass | time-lapse
  - transformation: optional, as start -> mechanism and speed -> end
  - action: a verb chain of up to about 35 words
  - visual: up to 8 words of light
- Scores: aliveness, spectacle, escalation, physicality, character, return, fantasy_legibility, renderability, truth, sound, voice_fit, hook, visitor_view. Drop counterpoint, visual_feasibility, concept_fidelity and length_fit as separate axes; fold them into voice_fit and the focus notes.

METHOD.md section 1 table:

- Add a new first row, "活起来": the good version is that the exhibit, or one part of it, acts with intent and returns to its place; the bad version is the camera pushing slowly over an object that never moves.

##### s3_shots_section
This replaces S3 paragraph 9 ("Shots. Two or three shots...") and the "Structure" sentences "Put the big jump between worlds at that cut...", "Each segment has one setting, with at most one jump to a second" and "Write the setting ... in at most 12 words". The new text:

Shots: direct the exhibit as the main character of a live-action fantasy short. The concept names the mode (awaken, world returns, character), the hero mover and its want; copy them into the script unchanged. The hero mover is exactly one thing: one sculpted part or figure of the exhibit (one ram, not four), or, in world-returns mode, the world and the object's contents while the object holds still. Name everything else that belongs to the object in still_parts; the builder states that they stay still.

Build each 15-second segment as three or four shots, each with a beat, in this order:
1. still: the real exhibit as the visitor knows it, camera locked off or moving slowly, with one tiny first sign of life by about three seconds (a hairline of light along a horn, one flake of patina dropping, a tick of metal, the case glass rippling).
2. stir: the inciting physical change, close on the part that wakes, written as a chain of verbs with visible consequences ("verdigris cracks off the brow and falls; the eyelid blinks; steam leaves the nostrils; the head snaps round to camera").
3. act: the big move at full force. The hero leaps, runs, climbs or flies, or the world floods, buries, rebuilds or races past the still object. Every motion verb carries a speed or force word (snaps, wrenches, leaps, lands hard, surges, a slow heave), and the shot says the physical result ("lands hard; dust bursts from the floor; the camera shakes on impact"). The video model defaults to gentle motion, so a segment without one high-amplitude shot fails.
4. return: back to the real exhibit at the angle of the visitor's photo, whole, still and correct in every detail, in the in-place view, with one trace of life left behind (return_trace: a last wisp of steam, a flake still settling, a faint glow fading). This is what the visitor matches when they look up.

Each shot has one framing, one primary camera move with its speed and endpoint, and one main action. Any move at any speed is allowed: locked off, push-in, pull-back, tilt, tracking (including fast and low), crane, orbit, handheld, whip pan with motion blur, crash zoom, FPV dive. A second move is allowed only as "<move>, then <move>" where the first is a quick transition (a whip pan, then locked off) or as timed beats ("0-2s locked off, 2-4s fast push-in"); never three. Orbit past about 90 degrees, or show a side the photo does not show, only if the shot lists a reference that shows that side. Write how each shot after the first begins in transition_in: cut, match cut on <named feature>, whip pan, through the rippling glass, or time-lapse.

Transformation. At most one transformation per shot, and give it at least four seconds: start state, then the change with its mechanism and speed, then the end state ("the patina peels back fast to bright gold bronze, then creeps back to grey-green"). The living hero and the vessel it belongs to may share a frame; that is the point of an awakening. Never use two references that show different versions of the same whole subject (the whole vessel newly cast and the whole vessel as it is now). The first shot of the awakened form in a segment lists its living-form reference; the living form is still the same material, with the same motifs, patina and proportions, now flexible. Keep inscriptions and fine decoration on the still object; never ask the moving hero to carry readable marks.

Write action as verbs and physics, never as adjectives: no magical, majestic, stunning, epic, cinematic, 8k. Spend words on the action and its consequence. visual is at most eight words of light or colour, and only when it adds something.

The picture is free, but it is fantasy, not testimony. Show the object waking or its world returning, never a reconstruction of a real event (its making, burial, discovery, damage, sale or ritual use with people) filmed as if it were documentary footage. In world-returns mode, enter the past through a transition device (the glass ripples, spotlights turn to firelight, a time-lapse) and leave by match cut. Show only what the facts make true in kind (a wine ritual for a ritual wine vessel, the kind of hillside it was found on), with no faces, no named people, no dated event and no specific place the sources do not give. A stylised look (ink-wash, shadow puppet, rubbing) is the other way to show the past, after a cut and in one named style. Never write the word documentary. Every shot of the real exhibit in the present matches how the pack and photo say it is displayed (case, plinth, hall).

Use only details visible in the photo or the references. Do not write @Image tags or restate the subject lock; the builder binds references from each shot's refs. Copy subject_lock, living_form_lock, subject_name and living_form_name from the asset plan unchanged. Write look as one concrete line (for example "photoreal live-action fantasy, gallery at night, warm spotlights"), or one named style.

Budget: each segment's request should stay at or under about 220 direction words (quoted narration excluded); the builder adds about 45 fixed words. Put the essentials first: the hero's action, then the camera, then sound. If you must cut, cut the sound list and light words before any speed, force or consequence word. Never ask for text, captions, signs, numbers or logos in the picture.

Structure of a 30-second film: two generations sharing the same identity and living-form references in the same upload order, joined by a cut. Put that cut on the landing or the return. Segment 1 ends on a near-still end_frame. Segment 2 opens as a continuation of the same character (not a recap) and runs its own STIR, then ACT, then RETURN. The setting field lists the place of each beat (gallery, then gallery again; or gallery, then ritual hall, then hillside, then gallery), one world per shot.

##### s3_narration_sound_changes
NARRATION (replaces S3 paragraphs 5 and 11 and the timing sentence in paragraph 17; the Truth paragraph 19-23 and Place words paragraph 15 stay unchanged):

Narrator. The voice belongs to the exhibit, or to its awakened hero. Describe it once, in one concrete sentence (apparent age, timbre, accent, pace, attitude), and repeat it word for word in both segments. narrator_presence:
- "voiceover" is the default.
- "on_screen" is for when the hero's face is in frame in CHARACTER mode. On a muzzle or a carved face, prefer voiceover over the next shot, because lip-sync on non-human mouths drifts.

Narration is a counter-track to the spectacle, not a lecture: 15 to 22 spoken words per 15-second segment, at most three lines, one short sentence each (at most about ten words).
- Leave the ACT beat (the leap, the landing, the transformation) with no voice, so its sound lands.
- The first line starts after at least 0.8 s of picture.
- The last line of each segment ends at least one second before the segment does.

Each line is one of two things:
- a sourced fact in the pack's own wording, or
- the character's voice with no claims (attitude, a dry aside, address to the visitor).

Plan one collision per segment: a plain fact that lands over an image that makes it astonishing ("bombs destroyed me" over the vessel standing whole with steam still curling from the ram that just ran). A line never captions its shot. A line never describes or explains the fantasy picture, and never says the fantasy happened ("I leapt", "I remember the hall"). The picture is imagination; the words stay true.

Keep from the old text: write for the ear, with numbers in words. Delete "That leaves roughly 25-30 words per 15-second segment".

Truth paragraph: add one sentence: "A claim that appears only in conflict notes, one secondary source or a verify flag is never voiced; write the safe line instead. Visual invention is never a truth failure, but a line that presents it as fact is."

SOUND (replaces S3 paragraph 25). Sound is the second actor. For each segment give:
- one ambience bed;
- three or four named foley sounds tied to the physical events, in the order they happen (bronze grinding on bronze, flakes tinkling onto glass, hooves clanging on stone with an echo, a hiss of steam);
- in world-returns mode, the world's diegetic sounds (torches crackling, frame drums, earth rumbling, rain) instead of a score.

"no music" is the default. If a score is part of the concept, name its instruments and tempo, and say which beat it hits (the awakening, the landing). Never leave the audio open: an unspecified soundtrack comes back scored. Never let music or speech continue across a segment cut.

The SFX field's description changes from "two or three" to "three or four, each tied to a named action".

##### FAILS_AT
- Stacked camera moves in one shot (for example orbit, crane and dolly-in at once) give jitter, drift or a direction change mid-clip. Cut, or write timed beats. (heyuan110; skills.sh seedance-camera)
- Cramming several locations or transformations into a short clip gives smeared morphing. Budget about one transformation per 4-5 s shot. (heyuan110)
- Instruction overflow: with about 8 requirements, only 4-5 get honoured. Keep each prompt to roughly 60-100 words (up to about 200 per multi-shot block) and put the essentials first. (heyuan110, Atlabs)
- A reference with no stated job bleeds its lighting, framing and pacing into the output. Two images showing different versions of the same subject cause identity flicker. (heyuan110, Magic Hour)
- Orbits reveal sides the reference does not show, and the model invents them. Supply extra views of anything that will be orbited. (Magic Hour)
- Very fast motion and complex contact: objects float or disappear, limbs deform, interactions look unnatural. The technical report also lists minor deformation artifacts, implausible motion in edge cases, high-frequency noise, and failures on out-of-distribution physics or composition. (WaveSpeed; Emergent Mind summary of arXiv 2604.14148)
- Imperfect fidelity to complex compound references: references condition the output but do not guarantee an identical object. Fine surface detail on a real artifact, such as inscriptions or exact decoration, can drift once it moves. (arXiv report; Magic Hour)
- Multi-subject consistency is weaker. Four rams moving at once is riskier than one hero ram. (official launch post, eWeek)
- Text rendering in frame is unreliable, especially with mixed languages or rare glyphs (seal script, for instance). Do not rely on the model for captions. (official post, heyuan110)
- Long dialogue drifts out of lip-sync. Multi-speaker lip-sync errors and occasional audio distortion. Unspecified audio comes back with music. (fal guide; arXiv)
- No negative prompts: write positive constraints instead. No motion-amplitude or reference-strength parameter on fal; the '75% reference strength' advice comes from other hosts. (Atlabs; fal llms.txt)
- First and last frame cannot be combined with omni references in one call. On fal, an exact end state (reassembly, for instance) needs the image-to-video endpoint and loses the multi-image references. (Magic Hour; fal image-to-video llms.txt)
- Hard limit of 4-15 s per generation. Extension quality is behind Veo 3.1, so a 30 s film should be planned as 2 generations sharing references. Identifiable real faces in references can be blocked. (fal docs; Moonlight review; heyuan110)
##### PROMPT STRUCTURE
Build each 15 s generation as a short drama beat, not a documentary pan.

(1) Reference binding header, one role per asset. "@Image1 = the real Four-Ram Zun exactly as photographed (identity anchor). @Image2 = character sheet of the living ram, front/side/back. @Image3 = the world it wakes into (optional). @Video1 = camera path only (optional)."

(2) One look line: a photoreal museum grade, or a single named style.

(3) Numbered, timecoded shots, 3-4 per 15 s. Each shot gets a size, one camera move with speed and endpoint, a subject action written as a verb chain with physical consequence, and a sound. Escalate motion amplitude across the shots: shot 1 grounded in the real exhibit (locked or slow); shot 2 the inciting change (crack, glow, blink, breath, head turn); shot 3 a big move (the ram leaps off, the gallery transforms, an FPV chase or a fast orbit); shot 4 resolution or return (a match cut back to the still bronze, the camera pulls out to the vitrine).

(4) Transitions stated explicitly: cut, match cut on the horn spiral, whip pan, pass through the glass.

(5) Audio: quoted lines at one sentence per shot, voiced by the character or a narrator; named foley; "no music" or a named score.

(6) Positive constraints, for example "@Image1 vessel shape and decoration preserved; one ram moves, the others stay bronze."

Keep each block to about 100-200 words. Split 30 s into two generations that share the same references, passing generation 1's last shot as @Video1 or an anchor still into generation 2. Amplitude comes only from wording (speed and force verbs, the "slow motion at 7 s, then snap to full speed" ramp), from the timeline, or from a motion video reference. Spoken facts must stay sourced. Visual invention should be readable as fantasy (waking, leaping, the world changing) rather than staged as a historical record, for example not showing a fake casting scene presented as documentary.
##### REFS
The most useful set for an exhibit-as-character film, up to 9 images on fal reference-to-video, each with an explicit @ role.

(a) The cleaned visitor photo of the real object. This is the identity anchor; state that the object keeps its shape, decoration and patina.

(b) A character sheet of the living form, made with an image model from the photo: front, 3/4, side and back views of the awakened ram or creature, keeping the same material and motifs but posed as a living animal. Add a face close-up. Plain background, nothing held. Add a side view if you will orbit.

(c) Optionally one in-motion keyframe of the living form, such as a ram mid-leap, so a large pose is anchored rather than invented.

(d) An environment or world plate for the transformation target (Shang workshop by firelight, an ink-wash landscape, the gallery at night). This locks continuity across cuts.

(e) Optionally one style plate, only if you are stylising.

Binding rules:
- Name each image's job in the first lines of the prompt.
- Refer to it again in the shot where it matters (\"Shot 3: the ram from @Image2 leaps...\").
- Never give two images that disagree about the same subject.
- Keep 2-5 images; more dilutes adherence.
- Keep upload order stable, because @Image numbering follows order.

For exact start and end states (fragments reassembling into the vessel, bronze turning to flesh), use fal seedance-2.0 image-to-video with image_url and end_image_url instead, since frame anchors cannot be mixed with omni references.

For continuity across a 30 s two-part film, reuse the same sheet and photo in both calls, and add the first generation's best frame or tail clip as an extra reference.
